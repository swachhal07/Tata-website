A municipal drainage contract in Birgunj is not glamorous work. Narrow lanes, live utilities, traffic that does not stop, and a schedule set by someone who has never stood in the trench.

The contractor ran a single Shinrai Power for the whole eleven weeks. We checked in twice during the job, and the useful lessons came from the operator rather than from us.

## Why a backhoe loader, not an excavator

On a drainage job in town, the machine does three jobs: dig the trench, move the spoil, and backfill and spread base material once the pipe or channel is in. An excavator digs well but needs a loader or tipper alongside it for the rest. A backhoe loader does all three with one operator and one machine, and it can drive between work fronts on the road rather than waiting for a low-bed.

The Shinrai Power is also built narrow on purpose, for the kind of street where a full-size machine cannot turn without blocking both lanes.

## What worked

The 4.7 metre backhoe depth covered every section of the drain without repositioning tricks, and the loader end did the backfill and the road-base spreading, so there was no second machine sitting idle waiting for its turn.

On the tight lanes, the ability to work from one side of the trench without a swing clearance problem mattered more than any number on the spec sheet.

## What changed after two weeks

- Bucket teeth swapped to a harder-wearing set once the old brick and rubble in the subgrade showed up.
- Grease schedule moved from weekly to every second day. Fine dust plus vibration is hard on pins.
- Start time shifted an hour earlier to get the noisy work done before the market opened.

## What other contractors can take from it

Most of what made this job run smoothly is transferable to any urban drainage or utility contract:

- Walk the route before you start and mark known services. Old town drains often sit alongside water lines and cables that are not on any drawing.
- Match the bucket to the trench. A bucket close to the finished trench width saves time trimming and backfilling.
- Expect the subgrade to change. Older streets hide brick, stone and old road layers, so carry spare teeth.
- Grease more often than the manual's minimum in dusty, stop-start work.
- Plan backfill as part of the same pass, so open trench length stays short and the street reopens faster.
- Agree working hours with the ward office and shopkeepers early. A quieter start keeps the job moving.

## The number that mattered

Zero unplanned downtime days across the contract. One planned service, done on a Saturday, at the site.

That is the whole argument for a machine with parts and technicians nearby. A backhoe that is 5 percent cheaper and 3 days late to fix is not cheaper.
